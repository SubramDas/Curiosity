# Student recruitment and validation plan

Created: 2026-10-11. Market: India, English. Proposed first segment: college students aged 18+ who used YouTube to explore a computing topic out of curiosity within the last month. This segment awaits founder agreement.

## Invitation draft — not published

> Are you an Indian college student aged 18+ who recently used YouTube to understand a computing topic outside required coursework? I am researching how students find explanations, decide what to learn next, and handle unanswered questions. I would like to hear about your most recent experience in a 20-minute English conversation. This is research, not a sales call. If interested, please complete this short form: [insert form link].

Only add an incentive after choosing one. A proposed ₹300–₹500 incentive for each of 15 completed interviews would cost ₹4,500–₹7,500. Pay for participation regardless of feedback; do not promise payment for a positive opinion.

## Form fields

1. Are you aged 18 or older? Stop screening if no.
2. Are you currently a college student in India?
3. In the last 30 days, did you use YouTube to learn about a computing topic out of curiosity?
4. What topic? What were you trying to understand?
5. Which other tools, if any, did you use in that same learning attempt?
6. Did you reach your goal? Yes / partly / no.
7. Are you willing to have a 20-minute English interview?
8. Optional contact information and permission to contact you for this research.

Collect only information needed for recruitment. Ask separately before recording interviews. Do not require an interruption or failed learning attempt for eligibility: include successful learners to avoid biasing the sample toward the problem.

## Interview guide

Ask about behavior before explaining the idea:

1. Walk me through your most recent attempt: where did you start and what happened next?
2. How did you choose an explanation? What made you trust it?
3. What did you do when you had a question?
4. If you switched tools, what caused that switch? Was it helpful or frustrating?
5. Did anything interrupt you? What happened afterward?
6. How did you decide you understood enough? Can you explain one thing you learned?
7. How many similar learning attempts did you make in the past month?

Then show the proposed journey and ask what they would use, what seems unnecessary, and what their existing tools do better. An expression of interest is not proof of use or payment.

## Record and assess evidence

Maintain a table with anonymized participant ID, recruitment channel, topic/goal, frequency, tools used, reason for switching, interruption, goal completion, and recurring unmet needs. Separate observed behavior from statements and interpretations.

Report counts with denominators. Fifteen recruited interviewees cannot establish prevalence among Indian students. Interview across more than one college or network where practical.

Next, invite 10 suitable participants to a curated prototype. Include three starting videos, a concept path, chat backed by reviewed material, related video suggestions, and brief understanding checks. Track learning goal completion, answer quality, effort, and voluntary return over one week. Compare with their normal workflow on comparable topics; avoid reusing a topic they just learned as the comparison.

## Suggested two-week schedule

- Week 1: prepare the form, recruit participants, and complete the first interviews.
- Week 2: complete roughly 15 interviews, summarize repeated patterns and counterexamples, and decide whether to run a prototype pilot.

The founder should lead interviews; the collaborator can handle scheduling, notes, and curation depending on skills and availability. No invitations have been sent and no external form has been created.
